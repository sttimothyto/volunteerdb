# Who can see what

The site shows each person what their own service needs, and no more. A
phone number is private. A parishioner gave it to the parish so that a
leader can call about a shift, not so that anyone can look it up. So on
every page the site asks what your role on that team is before it shows a
detail. Your role on one team says nothing about another team.

## Anybody can read this guide

This guide is open to everybody. You do not need an account to read it, and
the sign-in page offers it under the question mark. The guide holds no names,
no addresses and no phone numbers. The ministry home pages are open in the
same way. Everything else on the site asks you to sign in first. The
technical manual, written for the people who run the site, asks the same.

## Everyone who signs in

Everyone who signs in sees the *Dashboard*, the list of teams on the *Teams*
page, the *Events* page, their own profile and this manual. The *Volunteers*
page lists people, but a detail you have no right to see shows as *•••*.
*Elections* appears in the menu only while you sit on a voting roll, or lead
a team or are its second. A newcomer who serves nowhere yet can still find
the choir on the *Teams* page.

## A member sees names

A member of a team sees the names of the people on that team and their
roles, and nothing more. The email addresses and phone numbers do not
appear. A singer in the choir sees who else sings and can find them after
Mass, but does not see their phone numbers. A member of a large team does
not see the smaller teams inside it, either. To belong to the liturgy team
is not to see every altar server's name.

## A core member sees contact details

A core member helps run the team, so the full roster opens: names, email
addresses and phone numbers. The smaller teams inside it open too. The core
member who fills the Saturday shift needs a phone number to
fill it. A core member can also invite a person on the team to create an
account. The roster shows who has an account and who has not, and the core
member is the one who notices.

## A leader sees more, and can change it

A leader or a second-in-command sees everything a core member sees, and can
change it. They add and remove members, edit a member's contact details, run
the team's events and elections, and link the team's spreadsheet. They also
see each member's workload, described below. The same rights reach down
into the smaller teams, because that is how a parish delegates. The head of
liturgy also looks after the altar servers.

## Why a task force shows you less

A task force is a temporary team for one event, for example the parish
picnic, built from the members of several teams. To lead a task force is to
run its event, and nothing more. You do not see the borrowed members' phone
numbers or workload, you cannot edit their details, and you cannot invite
them. Those rights stay with the teams they came from. Otherwise any leader
who lent a team to the picnic hands that team's private details to whoever
runs it.

## Why you do not see your own workload

Workload is a number the site works out from how many teams you serve on and
how heavy each role is. A leader sees it for everyone on their own teams, as
a badge in green, amber or red by default. It is there so that a leader knows
before they ask an overloaded person for one more job. A member or a core
member never sees their own badge, and a core member does not see anyone
else's. A leader sees their own badge, because they sit on their own roster.
It is a planning signal for the people who hand out work, not a scoreboard,
and not a number to feel guilty about.

## Administrators

An administrator sees everything and manages the accounts, the custom
fields, the workload bands and the parish logo. A leader's rights come from
a team; an administrator's rights come from the account. The parish
secretary can be an administrator without a place on any team.
