# How elections work

When a ministry needs a new leader, the easy move is to ask the person the
priest already knows. The same dozen parishioners end up with everything,
and equally able people are never considered. An election puts more eyes on
the choice. It also shows what each candidate already carries before anyone
adds more.

## The seat and the proposal

A seat is one role on one team: the leader of the choir, or the
second-in-command of the food bank. The *Elections* page lists every team
without a leader or a second under *Vacancies*. A leader, a second or an
administrator clicks *Start proposal* there and names a first candidate. The
proposal carries 2 dates: the day nominations close and the day the vote
closes. Both days count to their end, so a ballot cast late on the last
evening still counts. The page shows the stage as a badge: *Nominating
until* a date, *Voting until* a date, then *Awaiting decision*.

## The roll of voters

The roll is the list of people who vote on this seat. The site fills it with
the team's leader, second and core members, plus every member of the team
named *Clergy*. The choir's own core knows who can lead the choir. The
clergy vote on every seat, because the appointment is finally the pastor's
act, and the pastor must be consulted on each one. A leader can add or
remove voters while nominations are open, and not after. A voter needs an
account to cast a ballot; the page marks *no account — cannot vote* beside a
name without one.

## Nominations

Until nominations close, any voter on the roll, and any leader or second of
the team, can put a name forward. Each nomination carries a short note under
*Why them?*. Under each candidate, *Current commitments:* lists every team
the person already serves on, so nobody is handed a third ministry unseen. A
leader also sees the candidate's workload badge. A candidate who is not on
the roll does not see the proposal at all.

## The ballot

When nominations close, the vote opens. Under *Your ballot*, each voter
gives every candidate a score from 0 to 5 and clicks *Submit ballot*. A
candidate left alone scores 0; there is no abstention. A voter can change
the ballot until the vote closes. Nobody sees a ballot but its owner: not a
leader, not an administrator. No totals appear while the vote is open, so
nobody votes with one eye on the count.

## How the ballot is counted

The site adds up each candidate's points. The 2 candidates with the most
points go to a final round. In that round each ballot counts as one vote for
whichever of the 2 it scored higher. A ballot that scored them equal counts
for neither. The candidate preferred on more ballots is the *STAR winner*.
The page shows the points of every candidate, the 2 finalists, and how many
ballots preferred each finalist.

Why 2 rounds? With a single mark, 2 well-liked singers split the choir's
vote and hand the seat to a third. Points do not split, because a voter
scores both. In the final round, a small, intense group cannot beat a broad
preference on points alone.

A tie is reported, not settled by chance. If the final round is tied, the
higher points total wins. If the totals are tied too, the page says *Tie
between* the names and names no winner. The same happens when a tie leaves
the site unable to pick the 2 finalists. That is acceptable, because the count only advises.

## The decision

When the vote closes, the page shows *Result*: the ballots cast, the points
and the winner. The count is advice. The final appointment is a human act: a
leader clicks *Appoint* beside a candidate, and it can be any candidate. Or
the leader clicks *Start new round*: the same candidates and the same roll,
new dates, and no ballots. That is the rhythm of an Ignatian election: pray
separately, vote separately, debate together, and repeat if needed. *Cancel
proposal* closes the seat without an appointment, and the ballots go with
it.

An appointment puts the candidate on the roster in the seat's role, exactly
as if a leader had edited the roster by hand. The history keeps it like any
other change.

## The digest emails

The site does not send an email at every step. Each night it sends at most
one email to each voter, with the subject *VolunteerDB elections: your input
is needed*. It comes on 2 occasions: you were added to a roll, or the vote
opened on a seat you vote on. Both restate the 2 dates. No email goes out
when a seat is appointed, and there are no reminders before a deadline. The
*Elections* page is where you find out.
