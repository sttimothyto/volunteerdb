# History and as-of dates

## Nothing is deleted

The site never forgets. When a leader takes a member off a team, or corrects
a phone number, the old fact stays on file. The record keeps the date and
the name of the person who changed it. The new fact goes on top. A removal is a dated fact,
not a hole: the record says that this usher served until March, not that he
never served.

## Any page can show a past date

The gear menu at the top right holds a box, *View as of (YYYY-MM-DD)*, and
a *View* button. Type a date there, and the *Dashboard* and the *Teams*
pages redraw as they were at the end of that day. So does a volunteer you
open from them. An amber
strip, *Read-only snapshot as of* the date, reminds you that you look at the
past. Nothing can be changed there until you click *Back to now*. The rules
about who can see what apply to the past as they do to the present.

## Why a transient parish needs it

A parish near a university or a hospital changes every year. Who led the
food bank team two Septembers ago? Who sang in the choir before the last
wave of moves? The as-of view answers from the record, not from memory. A
new coordinator can see the parish exactly as the last one left it.

## What the past is used for

The same record feeds the question the site exists to answer. On a
volunteer's page, *If they leave, what vacancies appear?* reads the roster
of today, and the *Service timeline* reads every past roster. A leader sees
who came back after a break, and who was promoted along the way. The audit
log, kept outside the site and read by the person who runs it, lists each
change with its date and its author.

## What the history does not keep

The history covers people, teams and roles. It does not keep old versions of
events, elections or the site's settings, so an as-of page shows today's
custom fields on yesterday's people. The date on record is the day the
change was typed in, not the day it happened in the parish. If a whole
roster was typed in on one afternoon, every member shows a start on that
afternoon. From then on, every change carries its true date.
